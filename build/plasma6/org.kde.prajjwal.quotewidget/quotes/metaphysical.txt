Title: That Which Is Unseen
Author: Kena Upanishad (1.5-1.6)

That which cannot be expressed by speech,  
but by which speech is expressed—  
That alone know as Brahman, and not this  
which people here worship.

That which cannot be comprehended by the mind,  
but by which, they say, the mind is comprehended—  
That alone know as Brahman, and not this  
which people here worship.

Title: The Wise Grieve Not
Author: Katha Upanishad (1.2.22)

अशरीरं शरीरेष्वनवस्थेष्ववस्थितम्।  
महान्तं विभुमात्मानं मत्वा धीरो न शोचति॥

(The wise man, having known the Self—  
bodiless within bodies, stable among the unstable,  
the great, all-pervading Lord—  
does not grieve.)

Title: The Bondage of Action
Author: Bhagavad Gita (3.9)

यज्ञार्थात्कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्मबन्धनः।  
तदर्थं कर्म कौन्तेय मुक्तसङ्गः समाचर॥

(This world is bound by actions other than those performed for the sake of sacrifice.  
Therefore, O son of Kunti, perform action for that sake (Yajna) alone,  
free from attachment.)
%
Title: The Undivided in the Divided
Author: Bhagavad Gita (18.20)

सर्वभूतेषु येनैकं भावमव्ययमीक्षते।  
अविभक्तं विभक्तेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि सात्त्विकम्॥

(That by which one sees the one indestructible Reality  
in all beings, undivided in the divided—  
know that knowledge to be Sattvic (pure).)
%
Title: The One Ruler Within All
Author: Katha Upanishad (2.2.12)

एको वशी सर्वभूतान्तरात्मा  
एकं रूपं बहुधा यः करोति।  
तमात्मस्थं येऽनुपश्यन्ति धीरा-  
स्तेषां सुखं शाश्वतं नेतरेषाम्॥

(He is the one Controller, the inner Self of all beings,  
who makes His one form manifold.  
The wise who perceive Him as existing in their own Self,  
to them belongs eternal happiness, not to others.)
%
Title: The Subtle Essence
Author: Chandogya Upanishad (6.12.2)

तं होवाच यं वै सोम्यैतमणिमानं न निभालयस  
एतस्य वै सोम्यैषोऽणिम्न एवं महान्यग्रोधस्तिष्ठति

(He (the father) said: "My dear, this subtle essence  
which you do not perceive—  
from this very essence, indeed,  
this great Banyan tree exists.")
%
Title: Truth Alone Triumphs
Author: Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.6)

सत्यमेव जयते नानृतं  
सत्येन पन्था विततो देवयानः।  
येनाक्रमन्त्यृषयो ह्याप्तकामा  
यत्र तत्सत्यस्य परमं निधानम्॥

(Truth alone triumphs, not falsehood.  
Through truth the divine path is spread out,  
by which the sages, whose desires are fulfilled,  
ascend to where that supreme treasure of Truth resides.)
%
Title: The Self Is All This
Author: Chandogya Upanishad (7.25.2)

आत्मैवेदं सर्वमिति।  
स वा एष एवं पश्यन्नेवं मन्वान एवं विजानन्नात्मरतिरात्मक्रीड  
आत्ममिथुन आत्मानन्दः स स्वराड् भवति

(The Self is, indeed, all this.  
He who sees this, thinks this, and understands this,  
delights in the Self, sports with the Self,  
unites with the Self, and has his bliss in the Self.  
He becomes self-sovereign.)
%
Title: The Uncaused Cause
Author: Shvetashvatara Upanishad (6.9)

न तस्य कश्चित्पतिरस्ति लोके  
न चेशिता नैव च तस्य लिङ्गम्।  
स कारणं करणाधिपाधिपो  
न चास्य कश्चिज्जनिता न चाधिपः॥

(He has no master in the world, no ruler,  
nor is there any mark of His.  
He is the cause, the Lord of the lords of the sense organs;  
He has no progenitor, nor any lord.)
%
Title: Asato Ma Sat Gamaya
Author: Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (1.3.28)

असतो मा सद्गमय।  
तमसो मा ज्योतिर्गमय।  
मृत्योर्मा अमृतं गमय॥

(From the unreal lead me to the real,  
From darkness lead me to light,  
From death lead me to immortality.)

Title: I Am the Self, O Gudakesha
Author: Bhagavad Gita (10.20)

अहमात्मा गुडाकेश सर्वभूताशयस्थितः।  
अहमादिश्च मध्यं च भूतानामन्त एव च॥

(I am the Self, O Gudakesha (Arjuna),  
seated in the hearts of all beings.  
I am the beginning, the middle,  
and the end of all beings.)
%
Title: Nothing Higher Than Me
Author: Bhagavad Gita (7.7)

मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय।  
मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव॥

(There is nothing whatsoever higher than Me, O Dhananjaya.  
All this is strung on Me,  
as rows of jewels on a thread.)
%
Title: The Light of all Lights
Author: Bhagavad Gita (13.17)

ज्योतिषामपि तज्ज्योतिस्तमसः परमुच्यते।  
ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं ज्ञानगम्यं हृदि सर्वस्य विष्ठितम्॥

(That, the Light of all lights, is said to be beyond darkness.  
It is knowledge, the object of knowledge, and the goal of knowledge.  
It is seated in the hearts of all.)
%
Title: The Eternal Among the Non-Eternal
Author: Katha Upanishad (2.2.13)

नित्योऽनित्यानां चेतनश्चेतनानाम्  
एको बहूनां यो विदधाति कामान्।  
तमात्मस्थं येऽनुपश्यन्ति धीरा-  
स्तेषां शान्तिः शाश्वती नेतरेषाम्॥

(He is the Eternal among the non-eternal, the Conscious among the conscious,  
the One among the many, who grants their desires.  
To the wise who perceive Him as existing in their own Self—  
to them belongs eternal peace, and not to others.)
%
Title: In Him Are Woven Heaven and Earth
Author: Mundaka Upanishad (2.2.5)

यस्मिन् द्यौः पृथिवी चान्तरिक्षम्  
ओतं मनः सह प्राणैश्च सर्वैः।  
तमेवैकं जानथ आत्मानम्  
अन्या वाचो विमुञ्चथामृतस्यैष सेतुः॥

(He in whom are woven the sky, the earth, and the interspace,  
together with the mind and all the vital energies—  
Know Him alone as the one Self.  
Dismiss other words; He is the bridge to immortality.)
%
Title: I Am Seated in the Hearts of All
Author: Bhagavad Gita (15.15)

सर्वस्य चाहं हृदि सन्निविष्टो  
मत्तः स्मृतिर्ज्ञानमपोहनं च।  
वेदैश्च सर्वैरहमेव वेद्यो  
वेदान्तकृद्वेदविदेव चाहम्॥

(I am seated in the hearts of all;  
from Me come memory, knowledge, and their removal.  
I am verily that which is to be known by all the Vedas;  
I am indeed the author of the Vedanta, and the knower of the Vedas am I.)
%
Title: All This Is Pervaded by Me
Author: Bhagavad Gita (9.4)

मया ततमिदं सर्वं जगदव्यक्तमूर्तिना।  
मत्स्थानि सर्वभूतानि न चाहं तेष्ववस्थितः॥

(By Me, in My unmanifested form, this entire universe is pervaded.  
All beings are in Me, but I am not in them.)
%
Title: The City of Brahman
Author: Chandogya Upanishad (8.1.1)

ॐ। अथ यदिदमस्मिन् ब्रह्मपुरे दहरं पुण्डरीकं वेश्म  
दहरोऽस्मिन्नन्तराकाशस्तस्मिन् यदन्तस्तदन्वेष्टव्यं  
तद्वाव विजिज्ञासितव्यमिति॥

(Om. Now, in this city of Brahman (the body), there is a small lotus, a dwelling.  
Within it is a small inner space. What is within that,  
that is what should be sought;  
that, indeed, one should desire to understand.)

Title: Thou Art Woman, Thou Art Man
Author: Shvetashvatara Upanishad (4.3)

त्वं स्त्री त्वं पुमानसि  
त्वं कुमार उत वा कुमारी।  
त्वं जीर्णो दण्डेन वञ्चसि  
त्वं जातो भवसि विश्वतोमुखः॥

(Thou art woman, Thou art man;  
Thou art the youth and the maiden too.  
Thou as an old man totterest along with a staff;  
Thou art born with Thy face turned in every direction.)

Title: The Self Is to Be Realized
Author: Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (2.4.5 Excerpt)

आत्मा वा अरे द्रष्टव्यः श्रोतव्यो मन्तव्यो निदिध्यासितव्यो

(The Self, my dear Maitreyi, should be realized—  
should be heard of, reflected on, and meditated upon.)

Title: The Eternal Self
Author: Bhagavad Gita (2.20)

It is not born, nor does It ever die;  
nor, having once been, does It cease to be.  
Unborn, eternal, permanent, and primeval,  
It is not slain when the body is slain.
%
Title: He Becomes That Which He Worships
Author: Brihadaranyaka Upanishad (4.4.5 Excerpt)

स वा अयमात्मा ब्रह्म विज्ञानमयो मनोमयः प्राणमयश्चक्षुर्मयः श्रोत्रमयः

(This Self is indeed Brahman, consisting of knowledge,  
of mind, of life, of sight, of hearing.)

Title: From Fear of Him
Author: Taittiriya Upanishad (2.8.1)

भीषास्माद्वातः पवते।  
भीषोदेति सूर्यः।  
भीषास्मादग्निश्चेन्द्रश्च।  
मृत्युर्धावति पञ्चम इति॥

(From fear of Him, the Wind blows.  
From fear of Him, the Sun rises.  
From fear of Him, Agni and Indra,  
and Death, the fifth, run.)
%
Title: The Worn-Out Garments
Author: Bhagavad Gita (2.22)

वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय  
नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि।  
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्य  
न्यानि संयाति नवानि देही॥

(As a person sheds worn-out garments  
and puts on new ones,  
so the embodied Self sheds worn-out bodies  
and enters other new ones.)
%
Title: He Who Sees All Beings
Author: Isha Upanishad (Verse 6)

He who sees all beings in the Self alone,  
and the Self in all beings,  
feels no hatred by virtue of that understanding.
%
Title: The Two Birds
Author: Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.1)

Two birds, companions (jīva and Atman),  
always united, cling to the self-same tree.  
Of these two, the one eats the sweet fruit;  
the other, looking on, does not eat.
